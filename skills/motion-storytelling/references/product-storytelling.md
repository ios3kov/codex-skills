# Product storytelling

Use for a commercial or product explainer whose value should be visible in actions. Carry forward the selected concept, actual capabilities, evidence boundaries, approved text and delivery constraints. This is a craft reference, not a separate generation or approval workflow.

## Make the product consequential

Distinguish noticing the offer from using it. A visible interaction might operate a control, apply a material, complete a software task, receive and act on advice, or use learned information. Show the relevant condition before the action and the meaningful response afterward. A product appearing beside a happy person does not explain that relationship.

Use observable outcomes such as an object changing state, a task completing, a decision becoming possible or a routine changing. Follow the same subject when that makes the relationship readable. A pressure/action/result sequence is useful for explanation, while a product reveal, fashion piece or mood film may have a different structure.

Choose a form that serves the argument:

- A demonstration needs the relevant operation and outcome to remain visible.
- A process visualization needs a consistent mapping between the depicted steps and supported facts.
- A repeated routine or organized sequence needs stable spatial relationships so progress can be recognized.
- Suspense needs a clear question and a payoff worth the delay.
- Material or fashion imagery needs detail and movement that reveal the actual object, rather than unrelated beauty shots.
- A fictional transformation needs a coherent visual rule and a recognizable product relationship.

These are options, not a fixed catalog of templates or mandatory shot counts. Support each shot's narrative job with framing, eye trace and sound using the existing references.

## Keep evidence honest

Record whether an image is an actual sourced demonstration, a supported explanatory reconstruction, or an illustrative scene. Generated footage, staged reactions, attractive textures and fictional effects do not independently establish product performance. A product fact can support an illustration; the illustration cannot create the fact.

For a real test, preserve the conditions, relevant state and visible operation needed to assess it. A hidden substitution or cut can invalidate the apparent comparison. Check that the depicted outcome stays within what the evidence supports; do not infer durability, comfort or physiological benefit from appearance alone.

Simplify the depiction without inventing capability. For exact UI, packaging or numbers, use supplied material or controlled editable overlays where needed and inspect the result. A metaphor should help understanding without implying an unsupported literal mechanism.

## Repair the argument

For a review, inspect actual picture and audio. Locate where the product is first recognizable, where interaction occurs, and which later event it enables. Identify the specific missing connection and the smallest shot or edit change that would make it clear. Establish whether the supplied clip is complete before calling an absent ending a defect.

For a script, carry shot IDs, action, changed state and evidence pointers into the existing shot plan. Let `editorial-copy` own claim wording, `media-production-control` own asset acceptance and provenance, and the selected tool workflow own execution. Do not add unavailable runners or automatic production handoffs.

## Authored interface demos

For a staged product demo, define the focal object, starting state, triggering action, dependent response and useful result before choosing motion. Give each scene a legible change; a short sequence is a starting point, not a required beat count or duration. Keep illustrations separate from evidence that the real app performs the depicted task.

Choose a settled result frame that explains the scene without playback. Use it for a poster or reduced-motion alternative when appropriate. Keep the result visible long enough to read; do not add movement just to occupy the hold.

In animated scenes, retain the spatial anchor while related content changes. Let dependent responses follow their cause, with overlap where readable. Keep text unwarped during surface resizing, preserve content through entrances and exits, and avoid abrupt replacement where movement carries the meaning. A loop should return coherently or show why its state resets. Do not apply presentation delays to real interactive feedback.

When authoring a cursor, resolve targets from current element geometry in the scene's coordinate system; remeasure after responsive layout changes rather than copying screen coordinates. Depict press, drag and release consistently with the target and state change. This is choreography, not a substitute for executing the real control.

Inspect normal-speed playback and samples around presses, menu openings, morphs, handoffs, exits and the loop seam. Look for clipped glyphs, shimmer, sudden jumps, stretched text, lost targets and unreadable final states. Check the actual narrow placement, pause/replay and reduced-motion behavior where applicable. A smooth deterministic capture does not establish smooth live playback; use measured frame timing when diagnosing a playback defect. Automated geometry findings are review candidates, not aesthetic verdicts.

Reuse the task's existing shot ledger for inspected widths, important transition times, observed defects and unperformed checks. Do not mandate a new rehearsal document, fixed sampling interval or screenshot count. Use approved existing capture tools; Stageshow's React kit and film scripts are not bundled or claimed as available. Interface composition remains with interface-craft.
