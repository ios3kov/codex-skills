# Shots and composition

## Analyze an existing sequence

Inspect source media, representative frames, shot boundaries, and available audio/transcript. A transcript does not establish framing or camera movement. A still does not establish a cut's timing.

Build a compact shot ledger with stable IDs:

| Field | Record |
|---|---|
| ID and timing | Start/end timecode, duration, source frame pointer |
| Composition | Attention, scale, negative space, symmetry, eye trace, depth |
| Lens impression | Visible compression/expansion/isolation; exact focal length only with metadata or reliable evidence |
| Movement | Subject/camera motion, direction, start/end framing, what changes |
| Cut | Boundary, action, sound, rhythm, incoming/outgoing visual relationship |
| Narrative | Information revealed or withheld and the shot's sequence-level function |

Label interpretations such as “likely isolates the character” separately from observations. Adjacent-shot evidence may support a sequence interpretation; do not present it as visible in the current shot. A useful test is what information or feeling a conventional framing/cut would lose. Report the resulting interpretation rather than an invented certainty about the director.

For many shots, batch frame extraction and organize the ledger before detailed analysis. Honor the requested coverage; agree any material reduction instead of silently omitting shots. Finish with the sequence's reusable visual rule.

## Stage a new shot

Plan attention over time, not just an attractive opening still:

- Choose the main subject and what the viewer should notice before and after the shot.
- Use grid alignment, contrast, scale, placement, and negative space to support that order. Thirds and centered symmetry are options.
- When depth helps, identify background/midground/foreground and camera-relative speed. Depth role and attention role need not be identical.
- Choose entrances/exits and movement paths that support narrative and eye trace. Account for locale and screen direction when those matter.
- Describe camera motion with property, start/end values, duration, curve, and anchor/focus. Combined motion is acceptable when it has a clear purpose.

A handoff includes pointers to the frame spec, focal coordinates, layer map, trajectories, readable holds, camera plan, and shot-to-shot continuity.

## Aspect and placement adaptation

Adapt hierarchy and framing for each requested aspect. Restack horizontal layouts for vertical when it improves readability; crop only when the approved content and framing survive. Do not choose a universal master aspect for every project.

Use current placement-specific safe-zone overlays or documented coordinates for critical faces, text, logos, and CTAs. Generic percentage margins are planning estimates, not guaranteed platform compliance. Inspect the final frame with the placement UI overlay and actual delivery size.

Check the beginning, peak, end, and cut-adjacent frames for clipping, off-screen entrances, eye-trace jumps, and legibility. A camera move must preserve important framing throughout its path.
