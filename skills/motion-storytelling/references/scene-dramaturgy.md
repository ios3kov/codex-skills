# Scene dramaturgy

Use when the story or dialogue needs development. Work inside the approved premise, genre, tone, characters, ending and deliverable. This reference supplies craft decisions within the selected workflow; it does not replace an invoked film pipeline's stages, production locks or approval process.

## Find the scene's change

Identify whose experience organizes the scene, what they want now, what resists them, and what success or failure would change. Stakes may be practical, emotional, social or comic; do not invent trauma or a hidden wound just to fill a character template.

Build actions and reactions that alter information, choices, relationships or control. A useful scene turn can be a discovery, refusal, mistake, decision, reversal or changed expectation. Show why the following event follows from this one. An atmosphere-led or observational piece may organize attention without a conventional conflict arc.

For an outline, track scene purpose, entering state, consequential action and leaving state. Retain only the detail useful at that stage. Acts, midpoint patterns, beat sheets and twist endings are optional organizing tools; a short scene does not need a feature-film structure.

## Write what can be experienced

In a screen story, express inner intent through behavior, objects, timing, speech and sound. Replace an unobservable thought with an observable cue when necessary, rather than adding explanatory dialogue automatically. Enter after irrelevant setup and leave after the meaningful change when that improves the scene; stillness or an extended aftermath can be intentional.

Make dialogue respond to what each character is trying to do. Distinguish voices through vocabulary, rhythm and tactics. Check whether a line negotiates, conceals, challenges, comforts or changes a choice. Subtext can enrich dialogue, while an honest direct statement may be exactly right. Read the exchange aloud when rhythm matters.

Use the requested script convention. A reader-facing story can keep camera language out; a shooting plan can specify shots and movement. Match a submission's actual format requirements rather than applying a universal camera-direction ban. Page count is a rough planning aid, not a measured duration: estimate from spoken delivery, action and pauses, then verify in a timed reading, animatic or actual edit as appropriate.

## Review at the right scope

Check continuity of motivation and knowledge, whether the turn is earned, what a viewer can infer from the supplied action, and whether the ending answers or deliberately leaves open the story's question. Suggest a concrete repair rather than adding generic spectacle. Preserve an explicitly requested unresolved or tragic ending.

Recurring appearance is owned by `character-continuity`; shot framing and edit timing use the existing motion references. Advertising promises and reasons to believe remain with `editorial-copy`. Developing a script alone does not start media generation.
