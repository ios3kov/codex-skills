# Editorial copy

Use one coherent editorial pass rather than applying several overlapping style checklists.

1. Resolve reader, purpose, language, voice and requested deliverable from the supplied context. For marketing, look for existing product-marketing context before asking about audience, offer and proof. Match a supplied writing sample.
2. Distinguish exact approved text from editable text. Draft from supported facts; name what the reader gets or needs to do. Examples and creative fiction can be invented when requested, but invented claims, testimonials and statistics cannot become factual proof.
3. Read the relevant mode only:
   - [Prose editing](references/prose-editing.md) for humanizing, rewriting and removing filler.
   - [Page copy](references/page-copy.md) for positioning, benefits, objections and calls to action.
   - [Ad creative](references/ad-creative.md) for angles, formats, variants and performance-led iteration.
   - [Creative mechanics](references/creative-mechanics.md) when concepts need different ways of making the message land, beyond new hooks or wording.
   - [Brand positioning](references/brand-positioning.md) for audience, category, competitive difference and reasons to believe; an ordinary copy edit does not require a brand audit.
4. Reconcile claims and voice after structural edits. For public-facing or publication copy, read [final factual wording](references/final-factual-wording.md). Deliver the finished text in the user's requested form; include annotations or alternatives only when useful or requested.

## Shared editorial decisions

Lead with the point. Replace vague praise with a concrete fact or remove it. Cut throat-clearing, staged revelations, invented oppositions, repeated punchline endings and forced lists. Vary rhythm when repetition obscures meaning.

In this prose-editing rule, staged revelations means formulaic rhetorical suspense that delays the point without a purpose. A deliberate reveal or supported visual contrast in a requested ad concept can serve its narrative; do not apply that device automatically to ordinary explanatory prose or an unclear page headline.

Preserve uncertainty and opinions where the genre calls for them. Active voice is a useful preference, not a grammatical prohibition. Adverbs, dashes, rhetorical questions and lists are context-dependent; the author's sample and meaning govern their use. Do not treat these patterns as evidence that a human or model wrote the text.

When editing a file, preserve code, metadata, commands, data and link targets unless their modification is explicitly in scope. A request to polish prose does not authorize replacing protected copy or summarizing required content.

For video ads, this skill owns message and creative hypothesis; `motion-storytelling` owns shot planning and timing when needed. Saving a draft does not authorize sending or publishing it.
