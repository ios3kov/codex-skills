# Final factual wording

Check the finished wording against the supported facts after editing. Preserve uncertainty, attribution, dates, numbers and causal strength. A smoother sentence must not turn “may help” into “proves” or a customer's claim into an independently verified result.

When factual meaning changes, rerun the affected verification with `fact-check` when available, or inspect the relevant primary evidence directly. Keep unsupported first-person experience marked for author confirmation; do not invent a testimonial to improve the copy. Pure opinions do not need factual proof, but their factual premises do.

Report unresolved material claims briefly. A style-only correction does not require a full new audit. Approval of a draft does not authorize publication.
