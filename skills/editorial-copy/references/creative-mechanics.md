# Creative mechanics

An angle selects the supported message; a mechanic determines how the viewer discovers it; a format supplies the execution. Keep the existing ad-creative workflow for claims, variants and experiments. Use this reference only when the concept itself needs development.

## Choose how meaning arrives

Start from the audience's situation, the intended takeaway and available assets. Choose a useful mechanism rather than assigning a tactic from awareness-stage labels alone. These options can travel between static, carousel, dialogue and video formats:

| Mechanic | Execution decision | Evidence boundary |
|---|---|---|
| Visual answer | Show an action or setting that answers the opening question without repeating it in narration | An implied benefit still needs the same support as a spoken claim |
| Changed perspective | Reveal a supported detail that changes how the situation is understood | Avoid inventing a false conventional belief just to overturn it |
| Observable contrast | Compare states, routines or processes with a readable visual relationship | Keep comparison conditions fair and product outcomes supported |
| Witness reaction | Let another person's response make a change noticeable | A staged reaction is fiction, not an actual customer endorsement |
| Conversational discovery | Let a question and response convey the offer in an appropriate voice | Do not present invented messages or participants as real evidence |
| Story-led reveal | Let useful content, tension or an action motivate the product's arrival | Respect the brief's identification and disclosure needs; no fixed reveal percentage |
| Category friction | Show a documented inconvenience that the offer addresses | Omitting a competitor's name does not make an unsupported comparison acceptable |
| Meaningful association | Connect the product with a relevant ritual, object, place or aspiration | Proximity does not establish endorsement, causality or performance |

These are creative hypotheses, not proven psychological effects or guaranteed conversion tactics. Combine mechanisms when the second one clarifies the same takeaway; a simple direct demonstration may be stronger than a layered device.

## Make alternatives materially different

For each proposed concept, record the audience takeaway, mechanism, key visible or spoken event, product's role, supporting fact or labeled invention, and production needs. Use the requested number and output form. Test whether removing the slogan still leaves a recognizable idea, and whether another variant changes the viewer's discovery rather than swapping adjectives.

Preserve required brand and offer information. A silent visual device may need an accessible equivalent in its actual delivery medium. Do not force humor, mystery, a conversational style or a missing CTA onto a brief that needs clarity.

Choose curiosity only where the format and requested task allow it. A marketing page headline should remain understandable without waiting for a reveal; shared prose-editing rules still govern ordinary explanation.

When turning a selected concept into a video, pass its takeaway and evidence boundaries to `motion-storytelling`; shot timing and production remain there. Use existing context or a reasonable draft assumption before asking for missing material.
