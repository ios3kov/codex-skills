# Brand positioning

Use for a requested positioning decision or brand analysis. Preserve approved identity and positioning during ordinary copy polish. Strategy supplies the argument for copy and visual direction; it does not authorize a new name, logo, promise or campaign.

## Separate intended identity and observed perception

Read the existing audience, offer, brand guidelines and evidence before choosing a framework. Record which statements come from the brand, which come from actual customer research, and which are strategic hypotheses. A website shows what a brand communicates; it does not establish what customers remember or feel.

Resolve the buying or use situation, audience, category or alternative being considered, useful difference, and basis for believing it. A concise positioning statement can connect these elements, but need not use a fixed sentence template. Distinguish attributes needed to qualify for the category from a difference that gives this audience a reason to choose.

## Compare relevant alternatives

Select the competitive set from the customer's decision, including a substitute, in-house process or doing nothing when relevant. Compare equivalent offers using the same dimensions: audience, use case, promise, evidence, constraints, channel and price basis where known. Verify current competitor facts from primary sources; attach dates and source pointers. Leave unavailable facts unknown.

A compact matrix often reveals more than a decorative map. If using positioning axes, explain why they affect the decision and how each placement was established. Call a map based on observable attributes an analyst's comparison; a claim about consumer perception needs consumer data. An apparently empty quadrant is a hypothesis about opportunity, not proof of demand.

## Turn analysis into decisions

Tie the primary promise to specific support and limits. Check consistency between ad, page, product behavior, service experience and visual expression. Separate factual proof from an emotional association or illustrative story. Prioritize a recommendation by audience relevance, support, feasibility and what would test it.

Use an established brand model when it answers the actual question; no minimum number of frameworks or competitors is required. Awareness, recall, perceived quality, preference and loyalty are different measurements. Report them only with a defined source, method, population and period; do not manufacture a 1–10 score or a weighted health index from impressions.

For a requested portfolio decision, compare the clarity, trust transfer, operating cost and reputation coupling of shared, independent and endorsed brands. Treat a proposed architecture or repositioning as a recommendation until the user authorizes changing protected assets.

Deliver the requested strategy, matrix or copy. Pass supported positioning and existing brand constraints to the relevant design workflow; leave visual treatment, typography and motion implementation with their established owners.
