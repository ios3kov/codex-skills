# Visual Direction

## Brief and incumbent truth

Inspect a representative route, real content, existing tokens, logo/type/color assets, and any supplied reference. Reuse an established design system before introducing another. Detect the framework and styling setup rather than imposing React, Tailwind, a font, or an icon package.

State the inferred direction briefly when it materially guides the work: surface, audience, purpose, and visual language. Resolve a material ambiguity about preserving the brand from the request and available evidence; ask only if the alternatives would change the result. A visual reference suggests composition or treatment, not permission to copy its content or invent product evidence.

For a redesign, distinguish:
- **Refinement:** retain recognizable identity, information architecture, and behavior; improve type, spacing, alignment, contrast, and hierarchy.
- **Replacement:** use a new visual language when authorized; preserve routes, required content, legal copy, analytics hooks, and functional contracts unless their change is also in scope.

Compare current screenshots/fixtures with the actual tokens and components before treating them as the incumbent design. A stale capture is historical evidence.

## Composition by surface

| Surface | Primary decision | Useful treatment | Common mismatch |
|---|---|---|---|
| Persuade | What promise and action should be understood first? | A strong hierarchy, concrete product evidence, purposeful section progression | Repeated feature cards with no differentiated message |
| Operate | What does the user need to see or do next? | Stable navigation, compact task groups, predictable state and location | Oversized marketing typography or ornamental motion that hides the work |
| Read | How will a reader scan, orient, and comprehend? | Clear headings, readable measure/leading, anchors, code/table handling | Large decorative interruptions or weak heading structure |
| Experience | Which artifact should lead? | Generous presentation of actual work, contextual captions, restrained chrome | A generic landing template that obscures the portfolio/gallery |

Set expression, motion, and density to suit the surface. These are independent decisions: a distinctive interface can have restrained motion, and a dense dashboard can have strong hierarchy. Do not import upstream numeric dial presets as requirements.

## Craft decisions

- Establish a type hierarchy using scale, weight, spacing, and line length. Use an existing brand face when available. Optical sizing, wrapping, and actual glyph coverage matter more than choosing a fashionable family.
- Use semantic color and spacing tokens. Reserve attention-grabbing accents for meaningful hierarchy or state; retain a brand's existing palette even when an upstream aesthetic rule dislikes it.
- Give sections a compositional purpose. Repeated cards are useful for repeated objects; avoid applying them mechanically to unrelated content. Do not hide necessary content merely to make the layout cleaner.
- Use genuine product imagery, supplied artwork, or an explicitly labeled illustrative/prototype treatment. Do not fabricate customers, metrics, testimonials, logos, or realistic product evidence.
- Use a consistent icon family and optical weight; give icons clear meaning. An existing library is a valid choice. Custom brand vectors remain valid assets.
- Motion should explain state, spatial relationship, or attention. Preserve usable content and controls with reduced motion. Route intricate gesture behavior to `apple-design` rather than inventing a second physics system.

Refine in a practical order: correct hierarchy and usability, align typography and spacing, reconcile color/material treatment, then add justified detail. A new dependency, elaborate scroll effect, second theme, or full component replacement needs a concrete task benefit.

## Product-specific expression

Test whether replacing the product name with an unrelated competitor would leave the composition and evidence unchanged. If so, connect the design to the actual subject, audience, material, and use scene rather than adding generic decoration. Preserve an incumbent identity during refinement.

For a Persuade or Experience surface, a memorable focal treatment can help the offer or artifact lead; choose it from the brief and real assets. It is optional, not a requirement for every screen or a limit of one focal area across a product. Keep Operate and Read surfaces suited to their tasks.

Numbered markers, labels, rules, and dividers should communicate real order, categories, or relationships. Use them when they clarify the content, rather than as compulsory stylistic ornaments. Strong direction does not require banned fonts, invented proof, or new dependencies.

## Critique and completion

Critique the surface against its purpose and real usage scene. Name the observed problem, its consequence, and a concrete correction. Distinguish a usability failure from a stylistic preference. Use real short, long, empty, and error content where those states exist.

For visual QA, inspect representative desktop/mobile captures together, fix the observed defects in a batch, and confirm once. This is a useful budget for polish, not a cap on required functional or accessibility verification. A discovered remaining defect warrants another focused pass.
