# Logo design and refinement

## Concept before finish

Choose the form from the name, audience, intended character and use context. Explore meaningfully different ideas when concept exploration is requested, rather than changing only colors or surface effects. Wordmark, monogram, pictorial/abstract symbol, combination mark and emblem are useful lenses, not a quota. A wordmark need not acquire a separate symbol; taglines and emblems are valid when the actual identity calls for them.

Explain each idea and its tradeoff. Inspect category conventions before calling a familiar metaphor distinctive. Do not impose a minimalist, luxury or technology aesthetic on every brand. Preserve existing equity during refinement unless replacement is authorized.

## Shape and negative space

Inspect the mark in neutral flat presentation before relying on color, volume or mockups. Examine silhouette, counters, joins, stroke rhythm, proportions and intentional asymmetry. Construction geometry can help coherence; no ratio, including the golden ratio, proves optical quality.

Inspect curves at large and intended delivery sizes for unwanted kinks, flat spots, uneven tension, accidental tangencies, tiny slivers, self-intersections, duplicate contours and clipping. Preserve intentional corners and connected forms. Balance the whole symbol/wordmark optically, not only its bounding boxes.

Choose vector editing, code-authored paths or generative concepting according to the task/tools; none guarantees quality. Inspect the actual result. For production vectors verify closure where needed, stroke expansion required by the importer, fill rules/holes, bounds and transforms. Preserve editable sources before destructive outlining/merging; separate shapes can be intentional.

## Lettering and lockups

Keep exact spelling, case, script, punctuation and diacritics. Inspect letterforms, counters, terminals, joins and repeated motifs. Kern pairs for coherent visual rhythm at relevant sizes; equal numerical gaps need not look equal. Custom changes must retain intentional readability.

Balance symbol and lettering, their gap, baseline and apparent weight. Deliver an approved lockup as one fixed composition rather than asking downstream users to reassemble its parts. Keep approved standalone marks and alternate lockups as named variants.

Use [shared typography](shared/interface-craft/references/typography.md) for font coverage, loading, features and rights. Availability or outlining does not establish permitted logo use; check the actual license when introducing a font. Provide outlined distributables when font-independent geometry is needed, preserving editable sources and font/version information where applicable. Never retype an approved outlined wordmark in a similar font.

## Visual similarity and recognition

For requested distinctiveness assessment, collect current direct-competitor marks from primary sources, recording dates and versions. Include relevant familiar marks when there is a concrete resemblance concern. Show comparable-size silhouettes, symbols and wordmarks side by side; examine negative space, letter structure, composition and category defaults, not only color.

Describe observed resemblance and affected features. Without the actual comparison set, leave competitive distinctiveness unverified and continue shape/application checks. A designer's assessment is not measured recognition or recall. Audience recognition needs a defined test, participants and observations. Visual comparison does not establish trademark clearance or exhaustive originality; undertake legal research only when separately requested.

## Application and delivery

Test actual small/large sizes, true solid single-color treatment where required, light/dark backgrounds, and real cropping/container shapes. Grayscale/tinted shading is separate: it does not prove suitability for single-ink cutting, engraving or embroidery.

Determine minimum size and clear space from observed behavior and brand constraints, not universal pixel/x-height recipes. Diagnose lost detail. A documented optical/small-size variant can be appropriate when authorized; never silently substitute it for the master. Not every wordmark must fit a 16-pixel favicon; not every brand needs every possible variant.

Render delivered files standalone: complete bounds, requested transparency, correct colors, no substituted glyphs/missing elements, and actual vector content in claimed vector output. Follow the intended medium's export requirements; avoid universal Apple RGB, CMYK, JPEG or fixed-package defaults.

Deliver requested sources/distributables with version, lockup relationships, permitted variants and usage constraints. Label sketches and illustrative mockups. Shape, brand fit, observed similarity and application suitability require separate evidence; presentation quality or an average score cannot erase a failed critical check.
