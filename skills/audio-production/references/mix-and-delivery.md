# Mix, stems and delivery

## Session and balance

Record the source version, picture lock/timebase, sample rates, output layout, target spec and available stems. Keep sources intact; edits and processed files are derivatives. Confirm sync at beginning, middle and end before processing away symptoms. A flattened mix limits what can be repaired independently.

Build from the intended foreground outward. For speech-led work level phrases/clip gain first, make room in music/effects through arrangement, gain automation or suitable ducking, then shape tone/dynamics where listening shows a need. For music-led work preserve the music's intended priority. A limiter cannot repair masked words or bad editing.

Apply EQ, compression, de-essing, denoising and reverb to diagnosed problems rather than fixed preset chains. Preserve consonants, timing, useful voice body and natural performance. Replace or repair a damaged phrase locally when broad processing worsens artifacts. Keep ambience through appropriate edits and intentional silence where the brief uses it.

## Measurement

The delivery contract determines integrated/program or dialogue-gated loudness, permitted peak, channels and other acceptance values. These methods are not interchangeable. Broadcast, platform and social targets can differ; record the actual current source of the chosen target or label it a rough-mix heuristic.

Use a suitable documented meter for integrated loudness, true peak and loudness range when required. Record tool/version, measurement mode, measured file/range and result. Sample-peak measurement does not establish true-peak compliance. Measure the encoded/transcoded delivery when lossy conversion can change peaks; do not transfer a WAV measurement automatically to an MP4 derivative.

Technical authorities: [ITU-R BS.1770](https://www.itu.int/rec/R-REC-BS.1770) specifies measurement algorithms; [EBU R 128](https://tech.ebu.ch/publications/r128) specifies a broadcast normalization framework. Consult the relevant current specification for actual numeric targets rather than copying an upstream cross-platform table.

## Stems and localization

Plan the full mix and needed dialogue/VO, music, effects/Foley/ambience or M&E stems. Match start/timecode, duration, sample rate and channel convention so they can be aligned without guessing. Keep language-dependent audio out of reusable M&E where dubbing needs it.

State whether stems are pre-master elements or intended to reconstruct a particular master. Nonlinear master-bus dynamics and sidechain routing can make separately processed stems differ from the full mix; verify the actual recombination contract and document processing/limitations. Do not promise exact summing solely from matching filenames and lengths.

## Actual playback and file checks

Inspect full playback for a final mix plus the relevant problem regions. Check speech masking, sync, clicks/pops, clipping, harshness, noise, room-tone gaps, loop seams and encoded artifacts. Test intended device classes such as headphones and phone/laptop speakers when available. For a stereo delivery check mono/fold-down when the destination or content needs it.

Confirm the actual export's codec/container, sample rate, channels, duration, expected streams and requested loudness/peak properties. Playback and measurement establish different claims. List inaccessible stems, unsupported meters, unavailable devices and unperformed listening as limitations, not PASS.

When a failure remains, fix source/edit/balance before mastering harder. After re-rendering, remeasure affected output and listen to the changed result; preserve the previous valid candidate for comparison and recovery.
