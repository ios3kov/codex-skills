# Sound spotting and Foley

Start from picture and script, not a generic list of effects. For each meaningful cue record shot/cue ID, time or frame range and fps, visible/narrative trigger, sound family, sync tolerance, perspective, material, layer role and source/asset pointer.

Useful families include feet, clothing, prop/contact sounds, ambience/room tone, designed effects, transitions, UI/product sounds, musical accents and intentional silence. Not every visible action needs an audible cue; protect the voice and leave room for emphasis.

Describe the cause, listener distance and desired emotional function. Translate vague adjectives into audible traits: transient, texture/body, tonal content, tail/reverb, stereo position, density and dynamics. Use scene scale/material/perspective to avoid a distant object sounding like a close cinematic impact.

For generated or sourced effects specify useful duration and handles, sync event, material/action, recording perspective, environment, whether a loop is needed, and unwanted content such as speech or music. Respect the provider's actual control surface; do not invent a seed, timing parameter or separated stems it cannot produce.

Keep important cues and beds separate when revisions, localization or exact sync need them. Align the audible transient to the event rather than assuming clip start equals contact. Shape starts/ends and test loops for clicks and obvious repetition. Use transitions when they motivate the cut; avoid placing a whoosh on every edit.

Hand off a spotting list, aligned assets/stems, source/prompt pointers, sync notes, approved variants and unresolved gaps. Meaningful non-speech cues needed for understanding belong in the caption plan. For layered playback check that foreground speech remains clear and that intended quiet moments survive.

When a cue fails, diagnose timing, material, perspective, density or source quality before replacing the whole soundtrack. Solo the affected layer, repair locally and listen again in the full mix.
