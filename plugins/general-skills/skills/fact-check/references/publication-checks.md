# Publication checks

Apply to the exact wording intended for readers. Prioritize load-bearing claims: a wrong or unsupported statement that changes the main argument, trust, reputation or a reader's decision. Keep opinions separate; check their factual premises only.

For first-person experience, distinguish author confirmation from independent evidence. If only the author can confirm it, mark AUTHOR CONFIRMATION REQUIRED and ask a focused question when material. A confirmation supports attribution, not independent verification. Never invent personal experience or treat missing evidence as proof of absence.

Check that editing has not changed association into causation, possibility into certainty, or a qualified estimate into a fact. Recheck affected facts, numbers, quotations, causal links and provenance after any factual wording changes. Do not rerun unrelated checks after a spelling-only fix.

For included media, inspect the actual source/version, stated license or permission, intended use and applicable consent. For generated media, record the generation origin and applicable disclosure requirements; generated does not mean rights-cleared. Check accessibility and disclosure only where relevant to the delivery. Missing evidence remains unresolved; do not guess a license or approval.

Return concise corrections and material unknowns. A missing material claim or rights check limits publication readiness; ordinary opinions and irrelevant checks do not create new blockers. Review approval never authorizes publishing or sending.
